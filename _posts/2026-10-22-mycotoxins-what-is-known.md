Mycotoxins are toxic compounds made by certain molds. The [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/mycotoxins) names aflatoxins, ochratoxin A and fumonisins among the mycotoxins that matter most for human health.

## The main exposure route is food

The WHO notes that people are mostly exposed to mycotoxins by eating contaminated food, such as grains, nuts, dried fruit and spices. For example, aflatoxins are classified as human carcinogens and can damage the liver. Food safety agencies monitor and regulate them for this reason.

## Indoor air: an active but unsettled area

Some molds that grow in water-damaged buildings, including *Stachybotrys chartarum*, can produce mycotoxins. However, the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html) states that there is no proof that mold causes conditions such as memory loss or a so-called "toxic mold" syndrome. The [Institute of Medicine](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/11011/damp-indoor-spaces-and-health) likewise found the evidence linking indoor mold exposure to many systemic effects inadequate or insufficient.

What this means in practice:
- The well-supported health risks of damp buildings are respiratory, such as asthma symptoms, cough and wheeze.
- Commercial urine mycotoxin tests marketed to consumers are not validated for diagnosing illness caused by building exposure.
- The fix is the same whatever the toxin: find and stop the moisture source, and remove contaminated materials safely.

## Bottom line

Mycotoxins are real and dangerous at high dietary doses. Whether indoor airborne mycotoxins harm health is still being researched. Treat visible mold as a moisture problem to fix, not a reason to panic.
