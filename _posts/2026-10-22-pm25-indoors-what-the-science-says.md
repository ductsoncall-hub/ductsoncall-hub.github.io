Fine particulate matter, or PM2.5, means airborne particles 2.5 micrometers in diameter or smaller. That is about 30 times smaller than a human hair. According to the [U.S. EPA](https://www.epa.gov/pm-pollution/particulate-matter-pm-basics), particles this small can get deep into the lungs, and some may reach the bloodstream.

## Why health agencies care

The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/pm-pollution/health-and-environmental-effects-particulate-matter-pm) links PM exposure to heart and lung effects, including aggravated asthma, decreased lung function and premature death in people with heart or lung disease. In 2021 the [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ambient-(outdoor)-air-quality-and-health) tightened its annual PM2.5 guideline to 5 µg/m³. In 2024, the EPA [lowered the U.S. annual standard to 9 µg/m³](https://www.epa.gov/pm-pollution/final-reconsideration-national-ambient-air-quality-standards-particulate-matter-pm).

## Indoor sources

Outdoor particles get inside, but homes also make their own. The [EPA's indoor PM guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/indoor-particulate-matter) lists cooking (especially frying and searing), candles, fireplaces and wood stoves, smoking, and some hobbies.

## Evidence-based ways to reduce exposure

- **Ventilate while cooking.** Use a range hood that vents outdoors and run it on the back burners.
- **Upgrade HVAC filtration.** The EPA recommends filters rated [MERV 13 or as high as your system allows](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/air-cleaners-hvac-filters-and-coronavirus-covid-19), and running the fan so more air passes through the filter.
- **Use a portable HEPA air cleaner.** Size it by its Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) for the room. See the [EPA's guide to air cleaners](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home).
- **Control combustion sources.** Avoid smoking indoors and make sure fuel-burning appliances vent correctly.

An HVAC system only cleans the air that moves through it. Leaky or poorly maintained ductwork can cut down how well filtration works, so it's worth having a qualified [HVAC and duct professional](https://ductsoncall.com) check your system.

## Bottom line

PM2.5 is one of the best-studied air pollutants. Indoors, the most reliable steps are controlling the source, ventilating and filtering properly.
